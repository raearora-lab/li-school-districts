Methodology and sources

Sources

  • Budgets, tax levies, tax cap, budget enrollment: NYSED Property Tax Report Card 2026-27 (May 2026 posting), with the 2026-27 revote file for five districts. p12.nysed.gov/mgtserv/propertytax
  • Enrollment: NYSED BEDS Day enrollment, 2025-26, with 2024-25 for the one-year change. data.nysed.gov/downloads.php
  • Pin locations: NCES EDGE public school district (LEA) locations, 2024-25: the geocoded district office address. nces.ed.gov/programs/edge
  • District boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line 2024 unified and elementary school districts, clipped to the county land area and simplified for display. census.gov TIGER/Line
  • Graduation rate: NYSED High School Graduation Rate database, 2024-25 release: 4-year outcome of the 2021 total cohort as of August 2025, all students, district level. Also Regents diploma with advanced designation and dropout shares. data.nysed.gov/downloads.php
  • Test scores: NYSED Report Card database 2024-25 (SRC2025): grades 3-8 ELA and math, percent of tested students at Level 3 or above, spring 2025, all students; Regents Algebra I and Regents English Language Arts, percent at Level 3 or above, 2024-25. data.nysed.gov/downloads.php
  • Teacher salaries: NYSED Personnel Master File statistical runs, 2024-25: median (50th percentile) salary of full-time classroom teachers, plus average salary per full-time equivalent and the classroom teacher count. nysed.gov personnel master file
  • Basemap: Esri World Dark Gray Canvas.

    Caveats

    • Revote districts. Locust Valley, Three Village, South Country, Islip and Bayport-Blue Point had May 2026 budgets defeated; figures are the revote proposals. Final outcomes are not in the file.
    • Central high school districts. Bellmore-Merrick, Sewanhaka and Valley Stream Central serve grades 7-12 only; their component elementary districts are listed separately, so per-pupil figures are not K-12 comparable. They have no separate boundary on the map.
    • Two enrollment counts. Students (PK-12) is the state's fall 2025 BEDS-day count of students in district schools. Per-pupil spending uses the district-reported budget enrollment, which can be higher where districts pay tuition for students taught elsewhere (Remsenburg-Speonk, Tuckahoe, East Moriches).
    • Budgets are proposals as submitted for the May 2026 vote, and include tuition, charter-school payments and debt service, which raise per-pupil figures in some districts.
    • Over the tax cap means the proposed levy exceeds the district's state tax-levy limit, which needs 60% voter approval.
    • No high school. 25 districts teach only up to grade 3, 6 or 8 and send older students to a neighboring or central high school district, so they have no graduation rate or Regents ELA figure. They are marked No HS, never estimated.
    • Suppressed scores. The state blanks a figure when a group is small enough to identify students. Grades 3-8 math is suppressed for 34 districts, mostly because a handful of 7th graders took the Regents Algebra I exam instead and the combined figure would reveal them; ELA is suppressed for 5 small districts. These are marked Suppr., never estimated.
    • Grades 3-8 math counts 7th and 8th graders who took the Regents Algebra I exam instead of the grade-level test, as the state does.
    • Teacher salary covers full-time classroom teachers only and only settled contracts; the state does not publish a salary where a contract was unsettled. A median for a very small district reflects only a few teachers.
    • Compare mode marks the best and worst value in each row in lime and pink where one direction is clearly better (higher scores and graduation, lower cost growth and per-pupil cost). Size, budget, levy and salary rows are only marked highest and lowest.
    • Pins mark district offices, not geographic centers; a few offices sit near a boundary (Westbury and East Williston are both in Old Westbury).
